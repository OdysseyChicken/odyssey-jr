What I learned today (Day 15)

Oven spring. In the first 10 to 15 minutes in a hot oven a loaf puffs up a LOT. The gas bubbles get hot and swell, water turns into steam, and the yeast does one last big burst before the heat stops it (around 140°F). Then the outside dries and sets into a crust, and the loaf can't grow any more.

Why cut it? The skin on the top is tight, so when it springs it has to tear somewhere. If you don't pick where, it bursts out the side wherever it's weakest (a blowout). A cut is a door you leave open on purpose.

The ear. The blade is called a lame (French for "blade"). Hold it low, about 30°, and slice under the skin: one side becomes a thin flap that lifts as the loaf grows, and because it's thin and up high it goes dark and crispy. That's the ear. In France the opened cut is called la grigne. Straight down (90°) opens flat into wide hills instead.

Steam. A lid (or my soaked oven door) keeps the skin wet and stretchy for the first ~20 minutes, so it can keep growing and the cuts can open. Then the lid comes off and the crust browns. No steam = the crust sets early, short loaf, dull crust.

Proof. Under-proofed dough still has lots of push left and tears itself open. Over-proofed dough has used up its bubbles and spreads like a pancake. Just right: when you poke it, the dent springs back slowly.

The white rings are rice flour from the banneton (the proofing basket). Rice flour doesn't soak in, so it stays white. My numbers here are simple pretend physics, but they behave like the real thing.
