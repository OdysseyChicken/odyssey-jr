How I learned it (the Koch method)

A long time ago a German psychologist named Ludwig Koch figured out that people learn Morse code faster if they never count dots. So the letters play fast from day one, and you learn each one as a sound, like a word. "Dah-di-dah" just IS K.

You start with only two letters, K and M. When you get 90% right, a new letter joins: R, S, U, A, P, T, L, O... in Koch's order, not A to Z. E and T come late on purpose, because they're so short they're too easy.

The timing is real: a dah is three dits long. The gap inside a letter is one dit, between letters three, between words seven. The word PARIS is exactly 50 dits long, so "20 words per minute" means 20 PARISes a minute. Here the letters play at 18 words per minute, with extra space between them (that's called Farnsworth spacing).
