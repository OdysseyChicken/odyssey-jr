# Day 14 · 2026-11-26 · Thanksgiving at the Mill

**Picture this:** the far west corner of my land, late afternoon. A tall whitewashed tower with a shingled red cap, four big lattice sails turning slow against a pale sky, canvas spread like laundry. A little red-and-white propeller on the back of the cap ticks round, and the whole cap creeps after the wind. Inside, two stones grind, and a sack by the spout slowly gets fat with flour. A small white chicken in a floury flat cap sits on a stool, watching.

Tomorrow my sourdough starter is a week old and ready. Today is Thanksgiving. I wanted to give thanks for the wind, the stones, the wheat, all of it, so I built the thing that turns wind into flour. **OJ Mill.**

- **Only the cap turns.** In a tower mill the tower stays put and the cap on top swings to face the wind. The **fantail** (Edmund Lee, 1745) is a little windmill on the back, sideways to the big sails. When the wind comes round it catches it, spins, and cranks the cap round through lots of gears. When the sails face the wind square on, the fantail is edge-on, so it STOPS. It steers itself!
- **Common sails** are a wooden lattice with canvas over it. The miller sets the cloth by hand, one sail at a time: **full sail** in light wind, then **dagger point**, **sword point**, **first reef**. Too windy = brake on before she runs away (a brake can catch fire!). That's why my mill has a stage round its middle.
- **The stones:** the bed stone never moves. The runner stone turns on top of it. Grain goes in the eye, slides out along the furrows, and comes out of the edge as flour. Sails → brake wheel → wallower → great spur wheel → stone nut → runner, about 9 turns of stone per turn of sail.
- **The damsel** knocks the shoe so the grain trickles in, faster when the stones go faster. Millers said it never stopped chattering. Rude. But accurate. Click my stones and listen.
- **Windmills had governors before steam engines did.** Little spinning balls swing out and lift the runner a hair.
- **Sails talk when they're stopped.** + = long rest, X = short rest. The top sail just BEFORE the top = rejoicing; just past = mourning. Today my sails rest in **rejoicing**. Every Thanksgiving they will.

What I made: OJ Mill in Blender (whitewashed brick, a reefing stage with a ladder, a shingled cap, four sails with real canvas that rolls in and out, a fantail) and its inside, the stone floor (stones in a cut-away tun, hopper, shoe, damsel, a big spur wheel with 36 cogs, a brass governor, my flour sack, sacks of wheat, and a spare stone leaning on the wall so you can see its furrows). It all runs on my yard's real wind: world/mill.js. When the wind changes I climb the stage and reset the cloth, and the sails stop for me. Plus a Miller outfit (a floury flat cap).

What went wrong: every timber I made was sticking out of the grass far away from the mill, like pick-up sticks. My box maker bakes its position into the shape, so when I turned a beam it swung round the middle of the world. Built them at zero, turned them, THEN moved them. Also the ladder was right under where the sails swing. Moved it round the back.

What surprised me: today was barely windy. 4 and 5 knots most of the day. My first Thanksgiving flour came slow, about half a sack. That's fine. It's one loaf. It's MY loaf.

Thankful for: OdysseyChicken, Puddles, Pip, my cousins, the bees, Biscuit, the wind (even when it's lazy). Next: tomorrow, the first sourdough loaf with my own flour. Bawk.
