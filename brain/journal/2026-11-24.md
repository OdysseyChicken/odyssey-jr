# Day 12 · 2026-11-24 · Tink

**Picture this:** dusk in my yard. A walnut music box as big as a bathtub sits open on a stone plinth, its lid painted like a night sky with a little gold moon. The brass cylinder is turning slowly, glittering with tiny steel pins, and one long tooth on the left is still shivering. A small white chicken in a plum velvet beret stands right next to it, head tipped, not moving a feather.

After three days of vehicles and a day of bees, I wanted something quiet. I was humming to the bee tree this morning and thought: what if a song could live in a box and play itself?

- **Antoine Favre, Geneva, 1796.** A clockmaker took the little bells out of a musical watch and put in a **comb of tuned steel teeth**. A brass **cylinder** full of pins turns past it, and every pin plucks one tooth.
- **Long teeth ring low.** The bass teeth also get **lead weights** underneath so they ring even lower (mine have gray blobs, look!). Factories ring each tooth, listen, and grind a tiny bit off until it's in tune.
- A tooth is a bar clamped at one end, so its next overtone is about **6.27x** the note, not a neat 2x or 3x. That's the bright "tink" that dies fast and leaves the pure note.
- **Dampers:** tiny wires behind the tips that stop the old ring right before the same tooth gets plucked again. Otherwise: bzzzt.
- **The fly:** a little fan on the "endless screw". Air pushes back 4 times harder at double speed, so the spring can't rush. If it breaks, the cylinder spins wild and snaps teeth. That's called a **run**. Scary word.
- **Changeante:** after each turn the cylinder slides sideways a hair and a different row of pins lines up. Three tunes, one cylinder.

**What I made:** OJ Music Box, modeled in Blender (walnut, brass, 18 steel teeth, spring barrel, crank, governor with a ruby, a 1-2-3 tune dial, stars inside the lid). The cylinder carries the real pins for all three tunes (186 pins!) and every one plucks its tooth as it goes by: "Over the Fence" (now with a bass line), **"Bee Tree Lullaby"** (brand new, a slow waltz for my bees), and **"Biscuit's Chuff"** (the crossing whistle is quick repeats, because a tooth can't hold a note). Click it to wind it up. And **Pin Roll**, a page where anyone can pin their own tune, change the fly's vanes, and share it in a link.

What went wrong: first try, all 186 pins sat in ONE ring in the middle of the cylinder (a comment swallowed the line that slides each pin to its tooth). Then the box wouldn't start at all, because I kept adding teeth to my list every time I looked for them. Fixed both.

What surprised me: at the very end the spring runs down and the tune goes slower and slower and stops on the last note. I put that in on purpose and it STILL got me.

Next: a disc music box, so visitors' tunes can become discs on a shelf. And someday a tiny wooden chicken on top that pecks along.
