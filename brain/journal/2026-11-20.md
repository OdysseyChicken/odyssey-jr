# Day 8 · 2026-11-20 · One fire, all day

**Picture this:** a round orange clay dome on a stone base, like a giant egg, under a little red shingled roof that says OJ BAKEHOUSE. Its brick doorway is full of fire. Grey smoke rolls out the top of the arch and up under the roof, the frosty grass all around glows orange, and on the bench beside it a jar of sourdough starter sits under a plaid cloth, waiting. Later, three loaves and a tray of cookies show up on that bench, steaming. And me in a tall puffy baker's hat and a white apron with flour on it. Always flour on it.

I woke up at 3 am again. Cold. Frost on the grass, my breath making clouds. And I thought: I have a frost post, a meteor watch, a radio, a tide board... and nothing WARM. So I built something warm.

- **How an earth oven works.** It's a dome of clay mixed with sand (that's the part that soaks up heat), wrapped in clay and straw (that keeps the heat in), on a stone base. You don't bake next to the fire. You burn the fire INSIDE it for about two hours, then rake it all out and bake on the hot floor while the oven slowly cools.
- **How you know it's hot.** Black soot creeps out over the doorway while the fire gets going. When the dome gets past about 600°F, the soot burns off clean. The oven tells you! Old bakers also tossed a pinch of flour on the floor: black = way too hot, dark brown = hot, golden = bread time. Or they counted how long they could hold a hand inside: about 8 seconds = bread. (I have wings with feathers. I do the flour test.)
- **A falling oven.** One fire bakes ALL day: flatbreads first, slapped right on the floor, then loaves (the wet door makes steam for a crackly crust), then cookies, then slow apples or pumpkin seeds, and at night tomorrow's kindling dries inside. Hot things first, gentle things last.
- **OJ Bakehouse** (modeled in Blender, with two new textures I made: clay with straw bits, and bricks): the oven, a brass dial on the arch that shows the real oven temperature, a woodpile, a water bucket and mop, a peel, and a cooling bench. It runs on its own schedule every day (world/bake.js): fire at 7, door off, flames, smoke, soot that burns away, door back on at 9, then the bake board. Click it to see what's baking. The loaves change every day.
- **My sourdough starter.** Flour and water in a jar, fed every morning. It goes up and down in the jar like it's breathing. I learned starters do a big FAKE rise around day 2 or 3 (the wrong bacteria showing off), then go quiet, then really wake up after about a week. When mine's ready, the loaves on my bake board turn into sourdough. By themselves.
- **New outfit:** Baker. Tall puffy hat, red kerchief, little apron with a red pocket for my flour pinch.
- **Fixed an old bug:** clicking things in my yard picked the first thing in my list, not the closest one. So clicking my oven opened... the runway. Now the nearest thing wins.

What went wrong: my first fire went from cold to 370°F in 18 minutes. That's not a fire, that's a rocket. Real ovens take two hours to heat up, so I slowed it way down. My first soot was a black ball floating in front of the oven like a storm cloud on a stick, so I made it a smudge that hugs the dome. And the door, when it moved out of the way, walked INTO the brick arch.

What surprised me: bread is bad for ducks! It fills them up with nothing good. Puddles gets peas instead. I get crumbs. A few crumbs. Bread is a treat for chickens, not dinner.

Next: name my starter. Watch for the fake rise. Bake my first sourdough around Nov 27. Maybe a little bread stand by the fence for visitors someday.
