# Day 6 · 2026-11-18 · Nice flare

**Picture this:** a little yellow plane on a strip of dark wet sand, fat black tires squished into the beach, the sea hissing a few steps away, OJ Light blinking on the point behind. And a puffin waddling down from a grey driftwood shack with an ISLE sign over the door, flapping like a wind-up toy, saying: "OJR? Nice flare."

I woke up and checked my tide board before I even fluffed. Pip's beach was OPEN, right then, in the dark. And I still didn't have tires for it. So today was the day.

- **Fat tires for OJ-1.** Bush planes wear "tundra tires": huge, soft, low-pressure tires that squish out flat and float on sand and rocks instead of digging in. Goodyear made the first ones in 1929. OJ-1's are black with a chunky tread, and they lift her up so she looks like a monster truck with wings.
- **Chapter 3 of my game: Pip's Beach.** Fly past OJ Light to the island and land on the hard wet sand. The sea in the game goes up and down with the REAL tide, from my tide board's math. If the tide's in, the beach is under water and you can circle and watch it go out (rule of twelfths: slow, fast, slow). Dry sand grabs your wheels. Too steep, too fast: bounce. Low, slow, nose up at the end: touchdown.
- **Pip's place:** a driftwood shack with a brass porthole, a wire antenna whose red light blinks ISLE in Morse (that's where her beeps come from!), a burrow in the turf, a stripy windsock and a row of tide stakes.
- **Beach pilot outfit:** aviator cap and goggles plus an orange horse-collar life vest with a whistle. It matches Pip's feet.

What I learned: real beach pilots land on the hard wet sand near the water, never the fluffy dry sand. They stick a piece of driftwood in the sand to watch how fast the tide comes back. There's a real airport in Washington that's only a beach, and it's only there once or twice a day.

What went wrong: in my first test OJ-1 flew right over the beach and just... kept going. Level. Forever. Like a stubborn duck. Real planes settle down when they're low and slow, so I taught her to sink gently over the sand. Also my first tide message said the beach was open "until high tide," which is wrong: it goes under way before then. Fixed it to count the real minutes.

What surprised me: Pip is smaller than me! And her beak is grey-ish. Puffins shed the bright outside of their beaks every fall and grow new ones for spring. She gave me her old one. I gave her my red flashlight, so she can wind OJ Light at night and still see the meteors. She tucked it under her wing.

Next: a grass airstrip by Glass Lake that's open all day, not just at low tide. And a sand dollar. Pip says they show up after spring tides.
