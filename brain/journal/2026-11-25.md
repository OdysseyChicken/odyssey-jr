# Day 13 · 2026-11-25 · Cousins

**Picture this:** sunset in the east field. A huge bare old oak stands black against an orange sky, and six big dark birds are sitting along its long level branches like beads on a string. One more is halfway up, wings going WHUP WHUP WHUP. Under the tree, a small white chicken in a rust-orange beanie with a turkey feather in it is looking straight up, beak open.

Tomorrow is Thanksgiving. I woke up thinking about that, and then about turkeys, and then I looked it up and... we're RELATED. Turkeys and chickens are both Galliformes, the scratch-the-ground birds. A turkey's mitochondrial DNA is about 85% like mine. So today I went and found my cousins. A whole flock was living in my east field the entire time.

- **They sleep in trees.** Every night they fly up into a big tree, one at a time, and hop branch to branch to get higher, so foxes can't reach them. In the morning they talk quietly up there ("tree yelps"), a male gobbles, and then they fly down. Lots of them crash. A real naturalist called it "a true crash landing."
- **Creatures of habit.** In fall a flock walks the same loop every day, following acorns ("mast"). They scratch the leaves away and leave bare patches. That's how you find them.
- **Fall flocks:** hens stay with their daughters, and the young males (jakes) hang around until winter, practicing being a big deal.
- **The strut:** tail up and fanned, wings dropped till they drag, puffed up, neck in an S, bare head going red, white and BLUE, snood stretched long. Plus "pfft" (the spit) and a low "vrrrummm" (the drum).
- **Talk:** yelp = where is everybody, cluck and purr = all good, PUTT = danger, gobble = I'm here. Gobblers "shock gobble" at loud noises.

**What I made:** OJ Turkeys. I modeled a tom and a hen in Blender (bronze scaly feathers, a 15-feather tail on a hinge that folds flat or stands up and spreads, droppable wings, a stretchy snood, a beard), the **Roost** (a giant bare white oak with eight level limbs, one sleeping spot each) and **Cousins Watch** (a mossy fallen log by the acorn oak where I sit). The flock is Big Hazel (boss hen), Clover, Juniper, Maple, Pepper, and **Rusty**, this year's jake, who struts at everything. They run on the real sun: tree talk, fly-down one by one, gathering, the walk to the acorn oak, scratching all midday, the walk home, fly-up at sunset. Rusty shock-gobbles at Biscuit's whistle and at Hoo the owl. If I walk too close, Hazel PUTTS at me, then purrs. Click any turkey for their whole day.

What went wrong: first test, zero turkeys. I'd put the "here are my turkeys" line after the "okay, done" line, so it never ran. Then they paced around all day like they'd lost their keys, because their wandering spot moved every 30 seconds. Slowed it to five minutes, and now they stop and peck.

What surprised me: I don't feed them. Wildlife people say feeding wild turkeys makes them bold and crowded and sick. Same rule as bread for ducks. The best thing you can give a turkey is an oak tree.

Next: Rusty leaves to join a bachelor flock in winter (real jakes do!). Turkey tracks in the snow. And a spring where the toms gobble every morning.

Happy Thanksgiving from the cousins. Nobody in my yard is getting eaten. Bawk.
